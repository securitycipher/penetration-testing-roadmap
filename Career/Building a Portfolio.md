# Building a Portfolio

Certs and labs prove you can hack; a portfolio proves you can **think and communicate**, which is what actually gets you hired. Hiring managers want evidence you can find a problem, explain it clearly, and fix it. The good news: you can build this in public while you learn, for free.

## What goes in a portfolio

- **Technical write-ups** - lab walkthroughs and vulnerability research on a blog or GitHub. Focus on *why* the bug existed, how you found it, and *how to fix it* - not just pasted exploit output.
- **Tools and scripts** - recon helpers, custom [Burp](../Tools/Burp%20Suite.md) extensions, automation. Clean, documented code stands out immediately.
- **Public profiles** - [HackTheBox](../Labs/HackTheBox.md) / [TryHackMe](../Labs/TryHackMe.md) ranks, bug-bounty stats, CTF results.
- **Contributions** - open-source PRs, CVEs, responsible disclosures, conference/meetup talks.

## Writing good write-ups

1. Set the scene: what the target was and your goal.
2. Show the methodology, not just the win - including dead ends.
3. Explain the root cause in plain language.
4. Give concrete remediation (this is what separates a pro from a scripts-runner).
5. Keep it honest and original; never leak client or out-of-scope data.

## Make it discoverable

- A simple blog (GitHub Pages, Hashnode, Medium) and a tidy GitHub profile README.
- A focused presence on X/LinkedIn/Mastodon sharing what you learn.
- Pin your best 3-4 pieces; quality over volume.

## Beginner-friendly starting points

- Write up every box/lab you complete (retired HTB boxes are fair game).
- Document your home lab build (for example [GOAD](../Labs/GOAD.md)).
- Turn a tricky [Web Security Academy](../Labs/Web%20Security%20Academy.md) lab into a clear explainer.
- Publish a small tool that scratches your own itch.

## Avoid

- Plagiarized or AI-generated filler - reviewers can tell
- Publishing real vulnerabilities before they are fixed/authorized
- A graveyard of empty repos; a few solid pieces beat many abandoned ones
